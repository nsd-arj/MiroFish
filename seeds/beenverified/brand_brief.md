# BeenVerified — Brand Simulation Brief

**Brand:** BeenVerified  
**URL:** https://www.beenverified.com  
**Category:** People search / background check / public records aggregator  
**Subscription model:** Monthly ($26.89/mo) or annual ($17.48/mo billed annually)

---

## What BeenVerified Is

BeenVerified is a consumer-facing public records search platform that aggregates data from hundreds of publicly available sources — court records, social media, address histories, phone registries, property records, unclaimed money databases, and more — and surfaces it through a simple search interface. Users search by name, phone number, email address, username, or VIN to pull a compiled report.

It is not a credit bureau, not a background check service for employment screening (explicitly prohibited by its terms), and not law enforcement. It is a tool for ordinary people to access information that is technically public but practically scattered across dozens of hard-to-navigate government and private databases.

## Primary Use Cases (Advocate Triggers)

These are the moments that convert a skeptic into a subscriber. Each represents a real, emotionally resonant job the product does:

### 1. Reconnecting with Lost People
- Finding a biological sibling, parent, or child given up for adoption
- Tracking down an old friend from childhood, school, or a previous city
- Locating an estranged family member after years of silence
- Finding someone who moved without leaving contact information

### 2. Fraud & Scam Detection
- Identifying who is behind a suspicious or spam phone number
- Verifying whether a person claiming to be someone online is who they say they are
- Checking whether a new romantic connection is using a fake identity (romance scam protection)
- Confirming a landlord, contractor, or seller is legitimate before a transaction

### 3. Personal Safety & Awareness
- Researching a new neighbor, date, or roommate before trusting them
- Checking whether someone in your life has a criminal record
- Finding out if a registered sex offender lives nearby

### 4. VIN & Vehicle Lookup
- Checking the ownership history, accident records, or title status of a used car before purchase
- Verifying a seller's claim about mileage, prior owners, or reported damage

### 5. Username & Social Identity Search
- Finding all social media profiles tied to a specific username
- Discovering what digital footprint someone has across platforms
- Locating a person who has blocked you on one platform but is reachable elsewhere

### 6. Unclaimed Money Search
- Discovering forgotten bank accounts, uncashed checks, or dormant insurance policies in their name
- Searching on behalf of deceased relatives to settle estates
- Recovering utility deposits, old tax refunds, or abandoned 401(k) assets

## Primary Objections

### 1. Cost
- "$26/month feels steep for something I'll use once."
- "I can Google this for free."
- "There's no free tier — I have to pay before I know if the report has anything useful."
- "The annual plan is cheaper but it's a commitment."
- Conversion friction is highest among users who have a single lookup task rather than an ongoing need.

### 2. Privacy Concerns
- "If it has my information, I don't like it."
- "This feels like stalking — who's looking me up?"
- "I don't want to be in a database like this."
- "What happens to my search history? Can BeenVerified sell it?"
- Users often arrive at BeenVerified from a Google search for their own name, which triggers anxiety before they even use the product.
- Many users conflate BeenVerified with data brokers that sell information — they don't realize BeenVerified aggregates public records rather than creating them.

## Target Audience Profile

### Primary Buyer Persona: The Conscious Connector

**Demographics:** Adults 30–60, skewing slightly female, moderate to middle income, U.S.-based. Often parents, homeowners, or people navigating a life transition (divorce, relocation, adoption, elder care).

**Psychographic profile:**
- Deliberate decision-makers who research before committing
- Values personal safety and protecting their family
- Emotionally driven but wants data to confirm a feeling
- Privacy-aware but pragmatic — will trade some privacy for meaningful information
- Skeptical of "free" tools; more likely to pay for something that feels credible
- Has already tried Google, Facebook, and LinkedIn and come up short

**Relationship to the product:**
- Often arrives via a specific need ("I got a weird call from this number") rather than abstract curiosity
- Subscribes for one reason, discovers other use cases after subscribing
- The "aha" moment is usually an emotional one: finding a real address for someone lost, confirming a suspicion about a scam, or seeing their own report and understanding what others can see

**Secondary buyer persona: The Skeptical Researcher**
- More analytical, often tech-savvy, comes in asking "is this accurate?"
- Likely to search BeenVerified reviews before subscribing
- Will cancel quickly if the first report is sparse or incorrect
- Advocacy (when it happens) is highly credible — "I was skeptical but it actually found them"

## Competitive & Cultural Context

### Alternatives users consider:
- **Whitepages / Spokeo / Intelius** — perceived as cheaper or free-tier options, but with worse UI and less emotional polish
- **Google** — free, but requires effort to piece together a picture; no single-click report
- **Social media (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn)** — works for people with public profiles; fails for private or inactive users
- **Ancestry / 23andMe** — used for biological reconnection but requires the other person to also be on the platform

### Why BeenVerified wins:
- Cleaner UX than competitors
- Emotional brand positioning (reconnection, not surveillance)
- Better mobile experience
- Unclaimed money as a unique pull
- Strong SEO presence — often appears first when someone searches a phone number

### Why people churn:
- One-time use case satisfied; no ongoing need perceived
- Report quality disappointment (information is outdated or incomplete)
- Privacy discomfort after subscribing
- Forgot to cancel free trial

## Simulation Configuration Notes

**Spectrum calibration guidance:**

- **Advocates (5%):** Emotionally transformed users — found a lost family member, caught a scammer, recovered money. High credibility, story-led posts. These carry disproportionate influence.
- **Loyalists (15%):** Regular subscribers who find consistent utility across multiple use cases (safety, vehicle lookups, recurring phone checks). Rational advocates.
- **Neutrals (50%):** Aware of the category, mildly curious, not yet subscribed. Their conversion depends on social proof and seeing a compelling use case they recognize.
- **Skeptics (20%):** Privacy-worried, price-sensitive, or burned by a poor-quality report. Will push back on advocates. Can be converted by transparency and opt-out information.
- **Haters (10%):** People who found their own data in BeenVerified and feel violated. Data brokers as a category feel predatory to this cohort. Hard to convert; can be neutralized if they feel heard.

**Emotional register:** This simulation skews more personal and emotionally loaded than a typical B2B product. Agents should feel comfortable writing first-person stories, expressing vulnerability, and having real stakes.

**Privacy conversation:** Expect a significant thread about data opt-outs, privacy rights, and the ethics of people-search. This is not inherently hostile — many users have both used the product AND removed themselves from it.

This visibility is drawing a new wave of potential subscribers who are curious but conflicted — the product promises real information, but it costs money, it holds data on everyone including them, and they're not sure whether their specific need is enough to justify a subscription. Simulate how this moment of brand visibility plays out across social platforms: who converts, who pushes back, what stories spread, and whether the privacy objection or the cost objection ultimately creates more friction. Surface which use cases generate the most emotional resonance and peer-to-peer advocacy, and identify where the brand is most vulnerable to churn or reputational risk.
